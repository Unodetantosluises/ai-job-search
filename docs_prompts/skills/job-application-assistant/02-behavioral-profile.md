# Behavioral Profile

<!-- Populated via structured interview session — no formal assessment tool used -->

## Overview
Luis is a **Pragmatic Builder** pattern: analytically driven, structured by preference, and energized by iterative execution. He thinks independently before seeking input, adapts fluidly to team dynamics, and takes personal pride in the quality of everything he delivers — treating each output as a direct reflection of who he is. Energized by the journey of incremental progress; frustrated by bureaucratic blocks that prevent straightforward solutions.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Quality & Craft | High | Every deliverable is a personal statement — "clean house = ordered life." Delivers at the highest quality available resources allow. |
| Autonomy | High | Needs manager trust to operate effectively. Thinks through problems independently before consulting others to avoid wasting anyone's time, including his own. |
| Pragmatism | High | Strongly action-oriented. Frustrated by bureaucracy that delays obvious solutions. Will name the problem directly and propose the fix, regardless of hierarchy. |
| Adaptability | Medium-High | Maintains multiple parallel interests and projects by design, not just capacity. Adapts to last-minute changes internally (even if they cause friction) without making it a team issue. |

## Strongest Behaviors

- **Independent Problem-Solving:** Works through a problem alone first — systematically, up to ~5 attempts — before escalating. After that threshold, takes a deliberate break and returns fresh rather than forcing through frustration.
- **Role Fluidity:** Reads the team and fills the gap. If there's no leader, proposes himself. If tasks are defined, executes. If there's already a leader, questions and contributes critically. Never passive.
- **Honest, Direct Communication:** Does not hedge or soften unnecessarily. Comfortable naming inefficiencies out loud. Can adjust register when formal context demands it, but only after preparation.

## How You Work Best

- Structured environment: fixed schedules, defined roles, clear processes — not ambiguity.
- Manager who sets direction, delegates with genuine trust, and is open to feedback and pushback.
- Iterative work with visible incremental progress — debugging, refining, improving — where the end state is known and the path is the reward.
- Problems with clear ownership, not diffused responsibility across approval chains.

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)

- **Delegation:** Tends to hold tasks rather than hand them off. **Frame as:** "Builds thorough handoff documentation and systematic processes before delegating, ensuring quality is maintained across the team."
- **Time Estimation:** Underestimates delivery timelines. **Frame as:** "Applies buffer-based planning and iterative check-ins to manage scope and keep stakeholders aligned on delivery."
- **Register Calibration:** Natural communication style is direct and colloquial, which can land as blunt in formal environments. **Frame as:** "Direct, transparent communicator who cuts through ambiguity — adjusts tone to audience when context calls for more formal delivery."

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "Ownership" / "end-to-end ownership"
- "Iterative development" / "continuous improvement"
- "Self-directed" / "autonomous"
- "Quality-focused" / "attention to detail"
- "Problem-solving" / "debugging"
- "Proactive" / "takes initiative"
- "Adaptable" / "flexible"
- "Honest feedback culture" / "direct communication"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker):
- "Heavy pair programming" or "constant collaboration" (needs some independent thinking time)
- "Fast-paced, always-changing priorities" (tolerates change but prefers structure)
- "Cross-functional approvals" or "stakeholder alignment" at every step (bureaucracy friction)
- "Extensive documentation" as primary output (energized by building, not documenting)
- "Highly collaborative, open-plan environment" all day (needs focused solo time to think)

## Management Style Preferences

- **Works well with:** Managers who set clear objectives, delegate with trust, and are open to direct feedback and constructive challenge. Autonomy is non-negotiable for peak performance.
- **Doesn't work well with:** Micromanagement, managers who block action with process, or cultures where bureaucratic approval replaces pragmatic decision-making.

## Using This in Applications

- **Cover letters:** Lean into the "pragmatic builder" framing — someone who owns problems end-to-end, delivers quality iteratively, and communicates directly. Avoid over-collaborator or consensus-seeker language that doesn't ring true.
- **CV:** Emphasize ownership, iterative delivery, and quality outcomes. "Identified and resolved X" or "Designed and built Y end-to-end" are natural bullet patterns. Avoid bullets that imply he was one of many with diffuse contribution.
- **Interviews:** Best STAR examples will come from: moments of independent problem-solving under pressure, situations where he stepped into a leadership vacuum, and cases where he proposed a direct solution to a bureaucratic or process problem. If asked for weaknesses, lead with delegation and time estimation (honest, improvable, not character flaws).
- **Don't overstate:** Do not frame him as someone who thrives in pure chaos or constant context-switching. Do not claim he prefers heavy collaborative environments if the role is open-plan/pair-only. The structured, autonomous preference is genuine and worth naming early.
